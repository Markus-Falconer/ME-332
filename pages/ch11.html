Ch 11: Heat Exchangers

Overall heat transfer coefficient, fouling, the LMTD method for sizing, and the effectiveness–NTU method for rating.

Jump to: ConceptsEquationsShort questionsLong problems

Key Concepts

  • Types: concentric tube (parallel flow or counterflow), cross flow (each fluid mixed or unmixed), shell-and-tube (shell passes, tube passes, baffles), and compact exchangers (lots of area per volume, usually finned).
  • Overall coefficient: $1/UA$ is the sum of every resistance between the two fluids: convection on each side, fouling on each side, and the wall. For a thin wall with equal areas, $U = (1/h_i + 1/h_o)^{-1}$. $U$ is controlled by the smaller $h$, so that's the side to add fins to.
  • Fouling (scale, rust, deposits) adds resistance $R_f''/A$ on each side. It lowers $U$ and therefore $q$ for given inlet temperatures. You detect it by comparing a measured $U$ (from test data) with the clean or design value.
  • Heat capacity rate is $C = \dot mc_p$. The fluid with $C_{min}$ has the larger temperature change.
  • Parallel flow: both fluids enter at the same end, and $T_{c,o}$ can never exceed $T_{h,o}$. Counterflow: $T_{c,o}$ can exceed $T_{h,o}$, and it's the most effective arrangement for a given $UA$.
  • LMTD method, $q = UA\,\Delta T_{lm}$: best for sizing, when the inlet and outlet temperatures are known (or follow from an energy balance) and you need $A$ or $L$. Define $\Delta T_1, \Delta T_2$ correctly for each arrangement. For multipass or cross flow, multiply by a correction factor $F$ applied to the counterflow LMTD.
  • ε–NTU method: best for rating, when the exchanger ($UA$) and inlet temperatures are known and the outlet temperatures are unknown. $q = \varepsilon C_{min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$.
  • $q_{max} = C_{min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$ is what an infinitely long counterflow exchanger would transfer. Only the $C_{min}$ fluid can go through the full temperature difference.
  • Phase change (condenser or boiler): the fluid's temperature is constant, so $C \to \infty$ and $C_r = 0$. Then $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-NTU}$ for every arrangement.
  • Diminishing returns: $\varepsilon$ rises quickly up to $NTU \approx 2$–$3$, then flattens. A bigger exchanger costs more and adds pressure drop for little gain.
  • Counterflow with $C_r = 1$: $\Delta T$ is the same all along the exchanger, $\varepsilon = NTU/(1 + NTU)$, and the LMTD is just that constant $\Delta T$.

Short Questions

S1 Outlet temperature limits

Can the cold fluid leave hotter than the hot fluid leaves, (a) in parallel flow, (b) in counterflow? Can $T_{c,o}$ ever exceed $T_{h,i}$?

Show answer
(a) No; the best case is that they approach the same temperature. (b) Yes. $T_{c,o} \gt T_{h,i}$ is never possible (second law).

S2 Why $C_{min}$ in $q_{max}$?

Why is $q_{max} = C_{min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$ and not $C_{max}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$?

Show answer

Energy balance: $C_{min}\Delta T_{min\text{-}fluid} = C_{max}\Delta T_{max\text{-}fluid}$. If the $C_{max}$ fluid changed by the full $\Delta T_{max}$, the $C_{min}$ fluid would have to change by more than $\Delta T_{max}$, which is impossible. Only the $C_{min}$ fluid can go through the full difference.

S3 Counterflow LMTD

Hot oil goes from 150°C to 90°C and water from 30°C to 70°C in counterflow. Find $\Delta T_{lm}$.

Show answer

$\Delta T_1 = 150 - 70 = 80$ K and $\Delta T_2 = 90 - 30 = 60$ K, so $\Delta T_{lm} = (80 - 60)/\ln(80/60) = 69.5$ K.

$\Delta T_{lm} = 69.5$ K

S4 Condenser

In a steam condenser $NTU = 2$. What is $\varepsilon$, and does the flow arrangement matter?

Show answer

The condensing fluid has $C \to \infty$, so $C_r = 0$ and $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-2} = 0.865$.

$\varepsilon = 0.865$, and the arrangement doesn't matter when $C_r = 0$

S5 Fouling

A clean exchanger has $U = 1000$ W/m²·K. After service, the total fouling factor is $R_f'' = 0.0005$ m²·K/W. New $U$?

Show answer

$1/U = 1/1000 + 0.0005 = 0.0015$

$U = 667$ W/m²·K (a 33% drop)

S6 Controlling resistance

A thin-walled tube has water inside ($h_i = 2000$ W/m²·K) and air outside ($h_o = 50$ W/m²·K). Find $U$. Where would you put fins?

Show answer

$U = (1/2000 + 1/50)^{-1} = 48.8$ W/m²·K, which is almost exactly $h_o$.

$U \approx 49$ W/m²·K; put fins on the air side

S7 Counterflow or parallel? like 11.20

Hot fluid ($C_h = 2$ kW/K) enters at 100°C. Cold fluid ($C_c = 1$ kW/K) is heated from 20°C to 75°C. Find $T_{h,o}$. Which arrangement must this be?

Show answer

$q = 1(75 - 20) = 55$ kW, so $T_{h,o} = 100 - 55/2 = 72.5^\circ$C.

$T_{c,o} = 75 \gt T_{h,o} = 72.5^\circ$C, which is impossible in parallel flow. It must be counterflow.

S8 Balanced counterflow

A counterflow exchanger has $C_h = C_c$ and $NTU = 3$. Find $\varepsilon$. How does $\Delta T$ vary along the exchanger?

Show answer

$\varepsilon = NTU/(1 + NTU) = 0.75$. With equal $C$ the two temperature lines are parallel, so $\Delta T$ is constant along the length.

S9 Which method?

For each case, pick LMTD or ε–NTU: (a) find the area needed to cool oil from 120°C to 70°C with water at known flow and inlet temperature; (b) find the outlet temperatures of an existing exchanger with known $UA$.

Show answer
(a) LMTD (sizing: all temperatures follow from the energy balance). (b) ε–NTU (rating: outlets unknown, so LMTD would need iteration).

S10 Making it bigger

A counterflow exchanger operates at $NTU = 4$ with $C_r = 0.5$. If you double its length, how much does $\varepsilon$ increase?

Show answer

At $NTU = 4$: $\varepsilon = (1 - e^{-2})/(1 - 0.5e^{-2}) = 0.927$. At $NTU = 8$: $\varepsilon = (1 - e^{-4})/(1 - 0.5e^{-4}) = 0.991$.

From 0.93 to 0.99: double the size for about 7% more heat transfer

S11 Mixed vs. unmixed

In a cross-flow exchanger, what do "mixed" and "unmixed" mean?

Show answer

Unmixed: the fluid is confined in tubes or fin channels, so its temperature varies across the flow direction as well as along it. Mixed: the fluid can move freely across the flow (like air over a bare tube bank), so its temperature is uniform across the flow and only varies along it. Real exchangers fall somewhere in between.

S12 Multipass LMTD

How do you apply the LMTD method to a 1-shell-pass, 2-tube-pass exchanger?

Show answer

Compute $\Delta T_{lm}$ as if the exchanger were counterflow, then multiply by $F$ from Fig. 11.10, which depends on $P = (t_o - t_i)/(T_i - t_i)$ and $R = (T_i - T_o)/(t_o - t_i)$. So $q = UAF\Delta T_{lm,CF}$, with $F \le 1$.

Long Problems

L1 Sizing a concentric tube exchanger (LMTD) like 11.18a

Engine oil ($\dot m = 0.3$ kg/s, $c_p = 2130$ J/kg·K) is cooled from 120°C to 70°C by water ($\dot m = 0.4$ kg/s, $c_p = 4179$ J/kg·K) entering at 20°C. The inner tube is thin-walled with $D = 30$ mm, and $U = 300$ W/m²·K.

  1. Find $q$ and the water outlet temperature.
  2. Find the required tube length for counterflow.
  3. Find the required length for parallel flow.
  4. Check (b) with the ε–NTU method.
Show solution

(a) Heat rate and water outlet

$C_h = 0.3(2130) = 639$ W/K and $C_c = 0.4(4179) = 1672$ W/K.

$q = C_h(120 - 70) = 31{,}950$ W, so $T_{c,o} = 20 + 31950/1672 = 39.1^\circ$C.

(b) Counterflow

$\Delta T_1 = 120 - 39.1 = 80.9$ K and $\Delta T_2 = 70 - 20 = 50$ K, so $\Delta T_{lm} = 64.2$ K.

$$A = \frac{q}{U\Delta T_{lm}} = \frac{31950}{300(64.2)} = 1.66\ \text{m}^2 \Rightarrow L = \frac{A}{\pi D} = \frac{1.66}{\pi(0.03)} = 17.6\ \text{m}$$

(c) Parallel flow

$\Delta T_1 = 120 - 20 = 100$ K and $\Delta T_2 = 70 - 39.1 = 30.9$ K, so $\Delta T_{lm} = 58.8$ K. Then $A = 1.81$ m² and $L = 19.2$ m.

(d) ε–NTU check

$C_{min} = 639$ W/K, $C_r = 0.382$, $q_{max} = 639(100) = 63{,}900$ W, so $\varepsilon = 0.500$.

Counterflow NTU relation: $NTU = \dfrac{1}{0.382 - 1}\ln\dfrac{0.5 - 1}{0.5(0.382) - 1} = 0.779$. Then $UA = 0.779(639) = 498$ W/K, so $A = 1.66$ m² ✓

$q = 32.0$ kW, $T_{c,o} = 39.1^\circ$C; counterflow $L = 17.6$ m; parallel $L = 19.2$ m (9% longer for the same duty)

L2 Rating an existing exchanger (ε–NTU) like 11.17

An exchanger has $A = 8$ m² and $U = 450$ W/m²·K. Hot water enters at 90°C and 0.5 kg/s. Cold water enters at 15°C and 0.8 kg/s. Use $c_p = 4190$ J/kg·K for both. Find $q$ and both outlet temperatures for (a) counterflow, (b) parallel flow.

Show solution

Setup

$C_h = 2095$ W/K ($= C_{min}$) and $C_c = 3352$ W/K, so $C_r = 0.625$.

$NTU = 450(8)/2095 = 1.718$ and $q_{max} = 2095(75) = 157.1$ kW.

(a) Counterflow

$$\varepsilon = \frac{1 - e^{-1.718(0.375)}}{1 - 0.625e^{-1.718(0.375)}} = 0.707 \Rightarrow q = 111\ \text{kW}$$

$T_{h,o} = 90 - 111{,}100/2095 = 37.0^\circ$C and $T_{c,o} = 15 + 111{,}100/3352 = 48.1^\circ$C.

(b) Parallel flow

$$\varepsilon = \frac{1 - e^{-1.718(1.625)}}{1.625} = 0.578 \Rightarrow q = 90.8\ \text{kW}$$

$T_{h,o} = 46.7^\circ$C and $T_{c,o} = 42.1^\circ$C.

Counterflow: 111 kW, outlets 37.0 / 48.1°C. Parallel: 90.8 kW, outlets 46.7 / 42.1°C. Same hardware, 22% more heat in counterflow.

Comment: in counterflow $T_{c,o} = 48.1 \gt T_{h,o} = 37.0^\circ$C, which only counterflow allows.

L3 Has it fouled? like 11.18b, 11.24

A counterflow exchanger with $A = 4$ m² was designed for $U = 120$ W/m²·K. In a test, hot oil ($\dot m = 0.25$ kg/s, $c_p = 2200$ J/kg·K) is cooled from 130°C to 80°C by water ($\dot m = 0.30$ kg/s, $c_p = 4180$ J/kg·K) entering at 20°C.

  1. Find the actual $U$ from the test data. Has fouling occurred?
  2. Find the fouling factor.
  3. What would the oil outlet temperature be if the exchanger were clean?
Show solution

(a) Actual $U$

$C_h = 550$ W/K and $C_c = 1254$ W/K. $q = 550(50) = 27.5$ kW, so $T_{c,o} = 20 + 27500/1254 = 41.9^\circ$C.

$\Delta T_1 = 130 - 41.9 = 88.1$ K and $\Delta T_2 = 80 - 20 = 60$ K, so $\Delta T_{lm} = 73.1$ K.

$$U_{test} = \frac{q}{A\Delta T_{lm}} = \frac{27500}{4(73.1)} = 94.0\ \text{W/m}^2\text{K} \lt 120$$

(b) Fouling factor

$$R_f'' = \frac{1}{94.0} - \frac{1}{120} = 2.3\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2\text{K/W}$$

(c) Clean exchanger

$NTU = 120(4)/550 = 0.873$ and $C_r = 0.439$. The counterflow relation gives $\varepsilon = 0.530$, so $q = 0.530(550)(110) = 32.0$ kW and $T_{h,o} = 130 - 32000/550 = 71.7^\circ$C.

$U = 94$ vs. 120 W/m²·K, so yes, it has fouled; $R_f'' \approx 0.0023$ m²·K/W; the clean oil outlet would be about 72°C instead of 80°C

L4 Cross-flow radiator, both fluids unmixed like 11.39

A car radiator (cross flow, both fluids unmixed) has $UA = 1500$ W/K. Coolant ($\dot m = 0.5$ kg/s, $c_p = 4200$ J/kg·K) enters at 95°C. Air ($\dot m = 1.2$ kg/s, $c_p = 1007$ J/kg·K) enters at 25°C.

  1. Find $q$ and both outlet temperatures.
  2. What $UA$ would be needed to reject 60 kW with the same flows and inlets?
Show solution

(a) Heat rate and outlets

$C_w = 2100$ W/K and $C_a = 1208$ W/K ($= C_{min}$), so $C_r = 0.575$ and $NTU = 1500/1208 = 1.24$.

$$\varepsilon = 1 - \exp\left[\frac{(1.24)^{0.22}}{0.575}\left\{\exp\left(-0.575(1.24)^{0.78}\right) - 1\right\}\right] = 0.594$$

$q = 0.594(1208)(95 - 25) = 50.2$ kW

$T_{w,o} = 95 - 50200/2100 = 71.1^\circ$C and $T_{a,o} = 25 + 50200/1208 = 66.6^\circ$C.

(b) $UA$ for 60 kW

$\varepsilon = 60000/(1208 \times 70) = 0.709$. Equation 11.32 can't be solved for NTU directly, so iterate or read Fig. 11.14. This gives $NTU \approx 1.92$, so $UA \approx 2320$ W/K.

(a) $q = 50$ kW, coolant out at 71°C, air out at 67°C; (b) $UA \approx 2300$ W/K, about 55% more for 20% more heat
